My Review of Austin, Texas

Published 10/13/2026

I have lived primarily in the Boston area for the past 21 years. I've come to the conclusion that it is, for better or for worse, a rather boring city. It lacks the night life of cities like New York, but it also has a lower cost of living, as well as an adequate public transportation system. People love to hate on it, and compared to the better systems in the country it can oftentimes be lacking, but its important to establish that one exists, and it is reliable 80% of the time. All in all, it is an area that I forsee myself continuing to live out my future, as it provides a good middle ground on the tierlist of major US cities. It's also where my Dad's family grew up, so it may just be my generational fate to be bound to this city for the rest of time.

Now, the title of this post is about Austin, not Boston. The above was really just a convoluted of way of concluding that I have been wanting to experience life in a different city for the better part of a decade, I just hadn't gotten the chance before. However, because of Northeastern's wonderful co-op program (wonderful maybe isn't the right word as it took about a hundred applications), I was able to land a co-op at IBM, and you'll never guess the location (hint: it's not Boston). Now, after about 1.5 months of living in a new city, I wanted to write down my thoughts on Austin in the likely event I want to look back on it while shovelling another 2 feet of northeast snow.

Truth be told, this was orginally just going to be a thinly veiled excuse to complain about the heat, as it is by far the worst apsect of this city. To me, an ideal outdoor temperature is mid 60s with a slight breeze, and slightly overcast because too much sun gives me a headache. Austin is changing me though, probably for the worst, as I now get excited when the temperature drops to a "cool" 80 degrees. The first week of September was especially brutal, with 100+ being the norm. It also didn't help that torrential rain swept the city not too long after, cooling things down back to the 80s, but also bringing with it a wave of miserable humidity. Walking to work that week was problably a low point in my life. I'm also convinced the sun is just evil out here. I was out with some friends during midday, and I believe the temperature had reached a sweltering 112. I think I may have experienced heat stroke for the first time in my life, because it was as if the sun was shining a laser directly into my brain and sucking all thoughts and energy left. Truly a unique experience for my life, and not a good one. I will concede that the weatrher has become surprisingly pleasant this October, with low humidity and temperatures sometimes even dropping to the high 70s. How lucky! Though normally I'd be expecting the streets to have frozen over into an icy hellscape by now, so it's still quite a chance of pace.

Now that I've been able to rant about the heat, I can say the things I have quite enjoyed during my time here so far. For one, the food is amazing, although prices are more expensive than I assumed, as I thought they would be significantly cheaper compared to Boston. The Tex-Mex places do make up the difference in their portion sizes however. A personal life tip is to never order an appetizer sample from Chuy's after already ordering a dish called "The King's Meal", or you'll be swimming in enchiladas and soft shelled tacos for the next 2 weeks. Besides my own personal mistake, the food tasted great, although I'm not exaclty a picky eater. Another standout type of food is the BBQ, with a special highlight on the brisket. The day I first tried some would be one of the greatest days of my life, if it wasn't the same day the sun tried to fry me into a crisp. Do try the brisket down here if you ever get the chance though, as it is worth the price. Austin also has a great amount of variety. This is probably due to Texas being a billion miles across, meaning they basically have infinite space to put a trillion stores with a quadrillion parking lots. Boston suffers from Massachusetts being a teeny tiny state, although it does make travelling the city easier. But, Boston does not and will likely never have a store called "Donut Taco Palace II", and it's worse off for it. It's a combination of depressing and amazing when it comes to the stores in Austin, as everything you could ever feasibly want is a 10 minute drive away, but the aforementioned evil sun has bleached it all overtime.

Finally, I have the utmost respect for HEB, my market basket away from home.

I'll be cutting this post short now, as I am writing this at 2am after realizing I haven't written in almost 9 months. I was orginially going to write a "Trying to write more blog posts" post, but it felt both a bit too meta and a bit too cliche, plus I already pulled that trick with my first post to get the ball rolling. I'll try to write a second post once my co-op ends in December, and see if my thoughts on the city have changed drastically by then. If anyone is still reading this, please enjoy the snow in my stead.
